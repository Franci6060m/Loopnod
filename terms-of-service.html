7. Messaging & End-to-End Encryption

Direct messages (text, voice notes, and shared audio clips) use a Double Ratchet encryption scheme with a full X3DH handshake, providing forward secrecy and post-compromise security. Group messages use a sender-key scheme, where each member encrypts once with their own key chain, which is rotated automatically whenever a Group's membership changes.

We provide a "safety number" verification feature for 1:1 conversations that lets you independently confirm you're talking to the device you think you are; we encourage you to use it for sensitive conversations.

By default, there is no message backup or account recovery for encrypted content, for either direct messages or Group messages: the private keys that decrypt your messages live only on your device(s), and we do not build any server-side mechanism to back them up, escrow them, or recover them. If you sign out, uninstall the app, or lose every device those keys were on — without chat backup turned on and with no other linked device to sync from (see below) — the messages that were only there are unrecoverable, including by us.

7.1 Chat backups (optional)

You can choose to back up your chats from Settings → Chats → Chat backup. This is off by default. When it's on, your backup is saved to your own Google Drive — a private storage area tied to your Google account, not to Loopnod — and we never receive or keep a copy of it on our servers.

  • End-to-end encrypted backup. Protected by a backup password or key that only you create and hold. We never receive, see, log, or store this password or key. If you lose it, the backup cannot be recovered — by you or by us. There is no "forgot password" option for an encrypted backup.
  • Standard backup. Protected using your regular account sign-in instead of a separate password, so there's nothing extra to remember — but it also means anyone who can access your account can access this backup.

You control how often backups run, and you can turn chat backup off or delete an existing backup at any time, from the app or directly in your Google Drive.

7.2 Linking a device shares your history

Linking a new device (Settings → Linked Devices) can bring over your past conversation history from one of your other active devices, instead of starting empty. This happens directly between your own devices and stays end-to-end encrypted along the way. If every device on your account is ever lost at once, with no device left to sync from and no chat backup turned on, that history is still gone for good.

Message and Group metadata — who you're messaging, who's in a Group and with what role, when messages were sent, and approximate file size/duration of voice notes — is not end-to-end encrypted and is visible to us as the service operator. See our Privacy Policy for the full detail on what we can and cannot see, including for chat backups.

We make no representation that this encryption implementation is suitable for protecting against nation-state-level adversaries or provides absolute security. You use the messaging features of the Service at your own risk, and should not rely on it as your sole protection for information where a breach would cause severe harm.
